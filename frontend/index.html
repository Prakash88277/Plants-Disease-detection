Abstract

India is an agrarian nation, with agriculture employing more than 70% of the population. Agriculture contributes to a percentage of our national revenue. Agriculturalists are losing money owing to numerous crop illnesses, and cultivators find it difficult to keep track of the crop on a regular basis when the cultivated area is large. As a result, plant disease identification is critical in the agricultural area. For the loss caused by agricultural diseases, which has a negative impact on crop quality and output, timely and precise disease identification is critical. At times, humans can’t detect the disease from an infected leaf with the naked eye. Disease detection is important because it may assist farmers in providing appropriate solutions to prevent this disease.

The deep-learning technique of leaf-based image classification using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), which has shown impressive results, can make the work of recognizing and classifying all diseases trouble-less and more precise. By leveraging transfer learning with EfficientNetB0 and ResNet50, the system achieves superior accuracy compared to traditional machine learning methods. The PlantVillage dataset is used for training and evaluation. Traditional methods typically rely on visual inspection by a human expert, which is time-consuming, less frequent, and can be inaccurate due to the limitations of human perception and experience.

EfficientNetB0 is often more efficient and can perform better, especially on tasks with limited data, while ResNet50 may provide higher accuracy on some benchmarks and benefit from larger datasets. To achieve the best results by combining both models in a hybrid approach, such as using them as feature extractors for a final classifier like a Support Vector Machine (SVM).
